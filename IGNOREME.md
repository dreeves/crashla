### Statistical Method

- The colored band around each MPI line is a 95% Bayesian credible interval. Model: incidents ~ Poisson(lambda * m), where lambda is the rate (incidents per mile) and m is VMT. Jeffreys prior: lambda ~ Gamma(0.5, 0) (improper). Posterior after observing k incidents in m miles: lambda | k, m ~ Gamma(k + 0.5, m). MPI = 1/lambda; quantiles are inverted via a monotone decreasing transformation.
- The credible interval combines uncertainty from incident counts (Gamma-Poisson) and from VMT by marginalizing the posterior over a two-piece (split) log-normal VMT prior with its mode at vmt and exactly 95% of its mass inside [vmt_min, vmt_max] (tail mass split in proportion to the side sigmas; degenerates to the symmetric log-normal when the band is log-symmetric — see splitPriorSigmas in crashla.js): the displayed lo/hi are the exact 2.5%/97.5% quantiles of that marginal — the same distribution the probability-distribution chart draws. (Until 2026-08-21 the bounds were instead a conservative worst-case pairing — vmt_min with the upper lambda quantile and vice versa — which held 97.9-99.4% of the posterior rather than 95%.)
- Which months count (since 2026-11-21; the human: "maximum conservativeness, don't show things when they involve arbitrary guesses"; "if we know when a helmer's crash reports end then we compute the exact VMT for the crashes. if not, we don't try to guess"). An NHTSA release holds the reports it received through the 15th of the prior month, rolled forward to the next business day (Oct 15, 2026 for the Nov-16 file), so its newest month, the data-through month, is only partly reported. A line's month enters the rates only when NHTSA's file is complete for its company and month (a month before the data-through month, outside the company's Monthly-report lag) or the company's own dated list covers it (Tesla's October 2026, below). Every other line-month at or before the data-through month is pending (today Waymo's and Zoox's October 2026): its miles are drawn faded on the VMT charts, like a ghost month's, and its crashes, checked and fault-rated like any other (17 Waymo and 1 Zoox today), are listed in the incident browser, grayed and labeled, and counted in no figure. A complete row of `data/vmt.js` has 1 in all six coverage columns (`coverage`, `coverage_min`, `coverage_max`, `incident_coverage` and its two edges); only a list-covered row has less. Until 2026-11-21 the data-through month counted at an estimated share of its miles (the share of its five-day-track incidents the release held, observed over expected, with a pooled incident coverage on top for Monthly-track metrics; for September 2026, as estimated on 2026-10-31 on the miles of that day, 0.1356, band 0.0615–0.52); over nine first releases what eventually arrived ran from 0.70 to 1.68 times that estimate, and the human dropped it.
- Preprints (since 2026-11-18): Tesla publishes its own list of the crashes it has reported to NHTSA with no safety monitor aboard, under NHTSA's Report IDs, and that list runs ahead of NHTSA's file (the list of Oct 18, 2026 held eight October crashes that the Nov-16 release, of reports received through Oct 15, did not). `data/slurp.py` reads the list beside NHTSA's files: a row whose report NHTSA's files hold is dropped (NHTSA's row stands), and the rest, the preprints, are counted like any other report, rated by the same blind raters, and marked in the incident browser until the release that publishes them. For the month the list runs through, while NHTSA's file is not yet complete for it, Tesla's two lines count the list's span (its crashes through its date over the miles driven through that date): the receipt coverage is Tesla's no-monitor miles through the list's date over the month's miles (Oct 1-18, 2026: 1,179,333 miles, Tesla's safety page's figure, which `quals/preprints.qual.mjs` holds `slurp.py`'s copy to since 2026-11-21; 53% of October on both lines; the all-modes line's monitor-aboard miles, which the list leaves out, count for nothing that month), and the Monthly-track incident coverage is the share of Tesla's Monthly reports that reached NHTSA on time (17 of 19, with its Wilson 95% lower bound, 0.69, as the low edge), since a list drawn up right after its last crash can lack one Tesla files late; on the five-day-track metrics (fatality, hospitalization, airbag, serious injury+) the list counts as complete. A crash of that month that the list lacks (a no-monitor report NHTSA holds and the list does not, or a report with a safety monitor aboard) is pending. Every list row NHTSA has published agrees with NHTSA's filing on its coded fields; one narrative of 24 differs by a word.
- A company whose Monthly-track reports reach NHTSA's file a release late (`MONTHLY_ARRIVAL_LAG` in `data/slurp.py`, measured from snapshot history; every Zoox Monthly report for Feb-Jul 2026 arrived a release late; every lag has been 0 since the Oct 15, 2026 file, which held Zoox's three August Monthly reports and seven of Tesla's eight but only 20 of Waymo's 43 (the other 23, and Tesla's eighth, came a release late, with the Nov 16 file); Waymo's lag stays 0 because a lag is the majority of the company's newest measured month, and September's 62 Waymo reports arrived on time, so a month whose Monthly reports arrive partly late has no model: AGENTS.md, open question 36) has the months inside that lag pending too (until 2026-11-21 they counted, thinned for the Monthly-track metrics by its measured 5-Day share: on the Sep-15 file, Zoox's July 2026 was at 0.63, band 0.25–1.0). A month's five-day-track reports are taken to be all in by its second release, which the saved releases bear out for every month since December 2025 but March 2026, whose second release was truncated.
- The point estimate shown in the line is the median of that same marginal posterior over 1/lambda, not the simple ratio m/k. For small k (especially Tesla), the prior pulls the estimate slightly downward; for large k (Waymo), the difference is negligible.
- The summary cards' multipliers (since 2026-11-17, the human's layout: "Injury: 155 incidents → 1,660,192 MPI (6.00x)" over "95% CI: 1,387,848 – 2,010,475 (3.15x – 11.5x)"): each is the median of R = (the line's MPI) / (the human MPI), the line's MPI its marginal posterior and the human MPI log-normal with the metric's Humans (AV cities) band as its 95% interval (as the distribution chart draws it), the two independent; the 95% CI line gives R's 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles in parentheses after the MPI's. R = (VMT / human MPI) / G, G the rate posterior's gamma, so R's CDF is the marginal CDF's integral over nodes in ln(VMT / human MPI), whose density is a mixture of two skew normals (ratioPriorNodes and multiplierInterval in crashla.js; multiplier-interval.qual checks them against the plain integral over the human normal). A multiplier is green when its interval lies above 1x, red when below, uncolored when it holds 1x. A line above the cards says what they divide by ("Multipliers are vs. Humans (AV cities)", the human's words). Until then a multiplier was the line's median over the band's geometric middle, colored by its value alone, and a card's only range of multiples was its All-incidents stress line, the line's 95% CI over the band's ends read as hard limits (the sensitivity table keeps that range): Waymo's at-fault injuries read 43.4x, with a range of 9.54x to 245x in that table, where R's median is 44.2x and its 95% interval 14.4x to 147x.
- Fault-weighted incidents (the at-fault metrics): each incident contributes its fault fraction (Claude's at-fault estimate) instead of one full count. Since the fractions are probabilities, the true at-fault count K is a Poisson-binomial random variable, and the posterior is the corresponding mixture over Gamma(K + 0.5, m) — reducing exactly to the plain posterior when every fraction is 0 or 1. (Fatality's 1/vehiclesInvolved fractions are deterministic allocations, not probabilities, and stay on the plain path.)
- **Tesla safety-monitor caveat:** Through May 2026 most of Tesla's robotaxi miles were driven with a safety monitor aboard (in the front passenger seat, and from September 2025 in the driver's seat on some trips: data/README.md, "Tesla's no-monitor line"); from June most were not (Tesla's no-monitor miles are 79% of June's deck miles and 88%, 94%, 97% and 98.5% of the July-October rows' centrals), so on the rows' centrals 36% of the default window's 6.485M Tesla miles (June 2025 through October 2026) had a monitor aboard, and 43% of the 5.42M of them the rates count (October only through the 18th, the span of Tesla's list, which holds no monitor-aboard miles: Preprints, above). (Until 2026-11-21 this note gave 54% of 4.241M, the window that ended at September.) Tesla filed 13 of the 17 in-scope crash reports with a monitor aboard as Driver/Operator Type "None" (the other 4 "In-Vehicle (Commercial / Test)"), and the monitors may intervene to prevent incidents. If so, the every-mode Tesla line's MPI is higher (better) than unsupervised driving would earn; the no-monitor line (since 2026-10-24), Tesla's own no-monitor miles and crashes, avoids this.

### Human Comparison Methodology

Human baselines are shown as shaded bands (range of plausible values) rather than single lines, and since 2026-06-11 come in three cohorts displayed as separate "helmers" (two shades of gold, plus orange for the default-off **Humans (Uber/Lyft)** proxy — the rider's alternative: the sourced Uber/Lyft fatality rates, since 2026-11-21 on the AV side's counting basis (the reports count a death in full for each platform car in its crash, so the rates are divided by FARS 2024's 1.54 vehicles per fatal crash: 163M–249M miles, where the published rates give 106M–161M), and elsewhere a band derived from the AV-cities one): **Humans (AV cities)** — the bands described below, surface streets in AV operating areas — and **Humans (US average)** — nationwide CRSS/FARS crashed-vehicle rates across all road types, Blincoe-adjusted at the low end. The true apples-to-apples MPI should fall within each band.
- **Scope by cohort and metric:** the AV-cities crash-frequency benchmarks (all / nonstationary / injury / hospitalization+ / airbag / serious-injury+) are surface-street rates in AV operating areas (higher crash rates than the national average), following the Kusano et al. and Waymo-hub approach, with the hospitalization+ and nonstationary adjustments taken from CRSS's urban non-interstate crashes; the AV-cities fatality band is all urban roads (freeway-inclusive, matching the AV side's all-roads scope); the US-average cohort is nationwide, all road types.
- **Hospitalization band:** The SGO's "W/ Hospitalization" (transported to hospital, incl. ## [AI TEXT] June and the Different Datasets

The NHTSA Standing General Order (SGO) analysis window starts **June 2025** (the default view) and rolls forward as NHTSA publishes new data, always extending through the most recent published month.
June 2025 is included as a full calendar month: SGO incident dates are month-granular, so a mid-month cutoff can't be applied to incidents, and the June 2025 VMT rows are full-month to match (the june-coverage qual checks the incident count is consistent with a full month).
coverage=1.0 in the VMT data means "VMT and incidents are already aligned" — no pro-rating needed; only a month that a company's own list covers carries a smaller value (the share of its miles driven through the list's date).
(Historical: the window was originally framed as June 15 – December 15, 2025, matching the first data pull; that framing survives in older notes above but the data has been full-June ever since the archive merge gave June full incident coverage.)
The data-through month (reports received through the 15th of the prior month, or the next business day) is only partly reported, and since 2026-11-21 no share of it is estimated: it is pending for a company with no dated list, and a list-covered month counts the list's crashes over the miles through the list's date, thinned for the Monthly-track metrics by the share of the company's Monthly reports that arrive on time (see the "Incident coverage for partial months" sanity check on the page). The cutoff itself is a reviewed constant in `data/slurp.py` guarded by content asserts; it is not inferred from the file's modification date, which runs a month later.

### The three datasets combined here

1. **NHTSA SGO incident reports** (the numerator).
   Two ADS CSVs — a "current" one and an "archive" for 2021–2025 — are fetched and merged by `data/slurp.py`, with NHTSA's current Level 2 (ADAS) and OTHER CSVs, read only for the one ADS-driven report NHTSA filed outside its ADS file (Waymo 30270-14303, coded "ADAS"; since 2026-10-31).
   Archival raw fetch snapshots live under `data/snapshots/`.
   The archive is needed because NHTSA keeps the reports filed under the General Order's earlier versions there (its SGO page, https://www.nhtsa.gov/laws-regulations/standing-general-order-crash-reporting, read 2026-10-31: the third amendment took effect June 16, 2025, and "Data files associated with prior versions of the General Order can be found in the archive section"), so June 2025's crashes are split between the two files, none in both: 13 in the archive, all reported in June 2025, and 59 in the current file, reported in June and July (every reporting entity's crashes).

These counts grow with each slurp run.

3. **AI fault-fraction estimates** (for fault-weighted MPI).
   For every incident, three blind raters each give the chance that an expert human driver in the AV's place would have avoided the crash, from 0 to 1 in steps of 0.05, and the fault value is their median.
   A rater is one headless Claude Opus call that sees a single crash report with the company's name masked and nothing else: no other case, no other rater, no earlier value, none of this repo (`data/fault-criterion.md` is its whole prompt; `data/faultrate.py` runs it).
   Nobody edits a value afterwards. Since 2026-11-17; until then agents rated the incidents in batches, with adjudication and a few overrides, and the values ran lower (Waymo's fault mass over the same 2,136 rows went from 172.55 to 422.90, Tesla's 31 from 12.70 to 14.55, Zoox's 54 from 3.00 to 11.10).
   Ratings are in `data/fault-ratings.csv`, values in `data/faultfrac.csv`.    These are used to compute fault-weighted incident counts.
   The scale is the probability that an expert human driver would have avoided the collision, and on door crashes the raters have one instruction, the criterion's rule 4: "Only the driving counts. What a passenger does is not the driver's doing, but where the driver stops to let a passenger out is." So a crash in which the AV's own passenger opens a door into traffic is rated on the stop, not on the passenger, and the values spread with it (30270-10336, parked at the curb, 0.05; 30270-16908, stopped partly in the lane, 0.3). Until 2026-11-17, under the method that rated in batches, these values were set by rule: an own-passenger dooring 0.1, or 0.3 when the door struck a cyclist, scooter rider or motorcyclist, the 0.1 being the driver's own part (an expert ride-hail driver sometimes prevents a dooring by stopping at the curb rather than beside a bike lane or moving traffic, and by warning the rider: the human, 2026-11-02, on what was then open question 25, since settled and deleted), and a closed door another vehicle's occupant opened into the passing AV 0 (from 2026-11-02, when the five such crashes still at 0.05 or 0.1 were re-adjudicated to the rule the other eleven followed). (Until 2026-10-31 this note said the three per-model judgments of early 2026 scored a passenger-caused crash 0; they split: of the own-passenger doorings of that day, Codex scored the ones it covered 0, Gemini 0 or 1, Claude 0 or 0.1-0.3. ### Scaling methodology

Since 2026-10-31, July 2021–March 2026 is reproduced by `python3 data/waymo_vmt.py` (from 2026-10-27 until then, July 2024–March 2026). For each interval between cumulative knots, the model subtracts measured California miles from the US increment. The residual includes the other states and California miles outside CPUC's reporting scope (and every mile before California's series starts in December 2022). Its daily rates are interpolated between neighboring intervals, then the monthly residuals are rescaled to preserve the interval total exactly. Measured California miles are added back; integer rounding cannot move a knot. The series start is a knot of its own, the pre-series offset. The April–June 2026 interval supplies the final slope while retaining its existing monthly rows.

This replaces the old claim that the US series was proportional to monthly California mileage. The old rows did not consistently implement that claim: California miles fell 33.1% in June 2025 but the US estimate fell only 4.4%; November–December 2025 California miles were flat while the US estimate rose 14.3%. The new method carries measured California changes directly and smooths only the unmeasured residual. It does not assume California's shutdowns occur nationally.

Until 2026-10-31 the July 2021–June 2024 monthly centrals were an older ramp and allocations, which did not follow California's monthly miles though their rationales said so (audit 2026-10-30, VMT-3). April–September 2026 uses the bottom-up construction below. October and November extend September’s daily rates. The full formula, calibration limitations, cumulative precision table and source links are in data/README.md, Waymo monthly allocation.

### Uncertainty bands

All authored `vmt_min`/`vmt_max` bands — whatever the ±X% shorthand in a row's rationale — are read by the app as the **exact 95% interval** (mode at `vmt`) of the two-piece log-normal VMT prior, per the S1 decision of 2026-08-21 (see splitPriorSigmas in crashla.js and the Statistical Method bullet above). So "±30%" means "the 95% band was authored at ±30%", not a hard bound or a 1-sigma band.

- **Jul 2021–Jan 2023**: Waymo's yearly rider-only totals (Kusano et al. 2025 Table 2: 0.328M in 2021, 0.524M in 2022) and its first million rider-only miles on Jan 21, 2023, less the pre-series offset, allocated with the same interpolation as later months; no California series exists before December 2022. The window starts at July 2021 because that is the NHTSA SGO reporting floor (the earliest incident in the dataset); the offset, the rider-only miles before it (range 0.15M-0.26M since 2026-10-31, central 0.205M since 2026-11-02), has no reportable-incident numerator and is excluded from the series. July-December 2021, 2021's lifetime total less the offset, carry the offset's range (162k-273k together); 2022's twelve-month interval, longer than the six months the allowance is calibrated on, keeps the old early allowance, half to twice a month's miles; January 2023 keeps Jan 1-21's daily rate for the month, Jan 22-31 at half to twice it. (A version of this ramp before 2026-07 overshot the ~1M milestone ~4x, seeding a constant ~2.8M level offset carried through Sep 2025; the ramp used from 2026-07-01 to 2026-10-31 met the ~1M milestone but put 4.71M through July 2023, 0.84M above Swiss Re's count, and 9.3M through 2023, 0.77M under Table 2's.)
- **Feb 2023–Mar 2026**: Measured California plus the residual allocated between knots: Swiss Re's 3,868,506 through July 2023, 7.14M through October 2023, Table 2's 10.066M through 2023, CSV1's 2024 totals, the paper's 56.7M through January 2025, then the hub anchors, with a floor at March 2024 ("Over 14.8M"). Uncertainty is on only the residual: log half-width `0.20 * sqrt((n - 1) / 2)` for an `n`-month interval, none for a single month. Knot uncertainty is included separately: source precision, the excluded-metro ranges at the hub anchors, and the pre-series offset's range. Forward and backward interval constraints reconcile the monthly and cumulative bounds. Calibration uses 30 California proxy months, January 2024–June 2026, with their monthly values masked and reconstructed from block totals; it does not validate national 95% coverage. The width is a fixed modeling prior, and the app interprets the final authored edges as its 95% prior interval. The formula and exact bounds are in data/README.md, Waymo monthly allocation. (Until 2026-10-31 only July 2024–March 2026 was built this way, with the June/July/December 2024 knots on the rounded milestones; February 2023–June 2024 kept an older allocation at ±35-50%.)
- **Apr–Jun 2026** (≈ −9%/+11%): Built bottom-up on the hub's measured Q2 (Sep 24, 2026 update; re-chained 2026-09-25): CPUC Q2-2026 CA driverless actuals (posted Aug 18, 2026; deployment + pilot, TotalVMTZEV) × 1.058, the ratio of the hub's four CA counties to CPUC in Q2 (1.040 in Q1; ±3%) + the hub's measured Q2 increments for Maricopa (11.57M) and Travis (5.28M) split by the prior monthly shape (±10%) + the hub's measured Q2 Atlanta increment (3.25M) + the excluded-metro estimate (1.4M/1.45M/1.8M; about −40%/+70%). Rows: 18.03 / 18.51 / 18.83M (the 2026-08-28 build had 17.4 / 17.85 / 18.1M on Q1 run-rates; the pre-rebuild rows, 19.0/20.5/20.5M, had assumed the CA share falling to ~47-50%). Cross-check: June ≈ 4.4M mi/wk vs Waymo's Jun 24, 2026 "more than 4 million miles every week". Jun held ~flat for the May-21-to-end-June US freeway-ride suspension. **Jul–Sep 2026**. Each month uses the Q3 filing's statewide California miles × the Q2 ratio (California component ±3%). For all three months Maricopa/Travis/Atlanta carry ±25%, and the newer metros outside the California estimate grow to 2.0/2.3/2.508M as Dallas opened to all riders Aug 4 and Houston Aug 20, Denver and Tampa admitted riders by invitation from Sep 1 and Las Vegas added riders gradually from Sep 14 (Waymo Help Center; Tampa opened to anyone on Oct 14), and San Antonio's passenger service resumed Sep 17. U.S. monthly miles are 19.67 / 21.78 / 24.04M (September less ~0.1M for the Phoenix weather pauses of Sep 16-18 and Sep 29). Freeway rides resumed Jul 29 from Phoenix, LA/SF Bay ~first week of Aug, Austin Sep 22; Ojai open to all riders Aug 19-20. The statewide filing includes San Diego, where invitation service began Sep 1 and service opened to anyone on Oct 14. San Diego's 3 of 25 September crash reports in the newer metros put its estimated share at 342,000 of their original 2.85M miles. The monthly bands allow for sampling uncertainty and differences in city crash rates (data/README.md, Waymo anchors). The next hub update covers data through Sep 2026 (~mid/late Dec).

### Notable events

- **Jun 2025 dip**: CPUC deployment + pilot data shows a 33.1% drop in CA VMT (from 5.355M in May to 3.582M in June); the rebuilt US estimate falls 16.3%, from 9.081M to 7.604M. This is explained by anti-ICE protests on June 8–9, 2025, during which protesters vandalized and set fire to Waymo vehicles in LA and SF. Waymo suspended service across both California markets for a significant portion of June, including expanded suspensions ahead of "No Kings" protests on June 14.

- **Pilot→deployment transition**: Waymo obtained its CPUC driverless deployment permit in Aug 2023, and deployment miles appear in that month's filing. In Dec 2023–Feb 2024, pilot VMT added ~12–16% on top of deployment. Pilot fell below 1% of the total in mid-2024 but rose to 6.8% in June 2026; both programs remain included.

- **Jun 2026 freeway recall**: Waymo recalled ~3,900 vehicles after its software failed to detect closed freeway construction zones in Arizona and California, driving into active work sites at speed (its sixth voluntary recall in ~2 years); freeway service was suspended pending a software fix, then resumed Jul 29, 2026, starting with Phoenix ([report](https://www.hngn.com/articles/271685/20260623/tesla-autopilot-crash-kills-texas-woman-waymo-recalls-3900-robotaxis-over-safety-failures.htm)). The current VMT series covers this: the suspension began May 21, 2026, and the Jun–Jul rows are held ~flat against it (freeway rides resumed Jul 29, Phoenix first). A second halt came in November 2026: the California DMV barred Waymo's driverless cars from freeways on Nov 11, after three of them entered a construction closure on U.S. 101 near Burlingame on Nov 5, and by Nov 17 Waymo kept its robotaxis off freeways in every market (TechCrunch, Nov 17, 2026; Fortune, Nov 18, 2026); the November row takes nothing off for it (data/README.md, Ghost months, says why). It remains a counterpoint to the headline 220.6M-mile safety framing — the Safety Impact comparisons are surface-street-weighted and do not capture this failure mode.
